Why Math Facts

Practice that does not feel like a test

I built Math Facts for my daughter, who finds her math facts hard. I wanted practice that would not make her feel bad about a wrong answer, so a miss gets a silly “Not quite!” and the right answer on screen, never a buzzer.

I also wanted her to see what she has learned, so every fact lives on a map that fills in as she masters it. And I wanted something real at the end of it, so grown-ups set the rewards, like an ice cream trip, and kids earn the stars. The treasure map was her idea.

Alexander Rohr, developer

How it plays

Short rounds, big rewards

Pick a player

Each child has their own player, with a buddy, a grade, a practice timer, and their own progress.

Answer on a big number pad

A round is 15 questions, with no timer unless you turn one on. The game learns which facts need more practice.

Learn from a miss

A wrong answer gets a gentle “Not quite!” and the right answer. Tap Show me how for a picture and steps.

Collect and celebrate

Earn stars, coins, level titles, achievements, and super buddies from the treasure map.

Treasure map

Eight islands, eight super buddies

Each island is a trail of stops, easiest first. A stop opens when the one before it is passed with 85% right, and the last stop is a treasure chest that wins the island's super buddy. Rounds on the map also count toward fact mastery, and they are never timed.

Features

Made to help a kid who finds this hard

Practice that adapts

Learns which facts each child knows and which need work, and spreads mastery over days so facts are remembered, not just answered.

Show me how

After a wrong answer, a step-by-step strategy with a picture: make a ten, near doubles, the nines trick, think times, and more, for every fact up to 12.

Wrong answers are no big deal

A missed fact gets a gentle “Not quite!” and the right answer on screen. Speed is a bonus, and there is no timer unless you turn one on.

Sign mix-up help

Notices when a child answers with the wrong sign, like 11 for 8 − 3, and points it out.

Big numbers in columns

Adding and taking away in columns like on paper, with carrying, borrowing, and the mistake shown in the right column.

Missing numbers

? + 5 = 12 and 3 × ? = 21: fact families the other way round.

Treasure map

Eight islands of stops to pass with 85% right, each ending in a treasure chest with a super buddy inside.

Achievements and levels

Nearly 100 achievements with progress to see, and level titles from Number Newbie to Grand Math Master.

Real rewards

Grown-ups set rewards like an ice cream trip. Children earn stars and choose where to put them.

Progress report

Practice time, accuracy, facts known, and the facts that need practice, for each child.

A profile for each child

Their own buddy, background, grade, practice timer, and progress.

Made for iPhone and iPad

Portrait on iPhone, and portrait or landscape on iPad, with the screens scaled up to fill the bigger display.

iCloud sync

Progress syncs through your family's own private iCloud. No account, no ads, no tracking.

For grown-ups

You steer, they play

Choose what to practice

Pick a starting grade from 1 to 4, then fine-tune which operations and which times tables or divisors to focus on. Changing these never erases progress.

Set the rewards

Create rewards with a star goal, like an ice cream trip or a movie night. The child sees progress on their page, and you mark it given.

See the progress

A report for each child shows practice time, accuracy, week-by-week growth, facts known, and the facts that keep tripping them up.

Behind a parental gate

The Grown-ups area, the purchase screen, and links out of the app are behind a gate: type a big number from its spelled-out words. Children never see prices or buy buttons.
