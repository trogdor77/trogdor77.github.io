The spark

Why Scorchlings exists

Scorchlings is a color shooter puzzle made to be played without interruptions: no ads between levels, no ads to continue, and no ads ever.

The dragons tie in with the Rohr Software logo, a dragon's eye. Every one of the 1,000 levels was generated and then checked by a solver that plays it with the game's own rules, so every level can be won without power-ups. All of the art is drawn in code.

Alexander Rohr, founder and developer

The flight

How it plays

Send a dragon

Tap the dragon at the front of a column to send it around the picture on the belt.

Burn its color

At each spot it breathes at the first tile it can see, burning it if the colors match.

Roost or fly home

Dragons out of fire fly home. Dragons with fire left land in a roost to go again later.

Clear the picture

Burn every tile to win. Run out of roosts and the level is lost.

The hoard

What it does

1,000 solver-checked levels

Pictures grow from small sprites to full boards with light and dark shades of the same color. Hard and super hard levels are marked on the map.

Twists

Chained dragons that fly together, mystery dragons that hide their color, frozen dragons that thaw as you play, and stone walls that block fire.

Five power-ups

Undo, Pluck (send any queued dragon next), Fireball (a meteor that blasts a 3×3 patch), Unchain and Extra Roost.

Daily Treasure

A seven-day gift calendar that pauses instead of resetting, Hearth Fire for unlimited lives, and double embers on the first win of each day.

Game Center

Three leaderboards (levels cleared, embers earned and best win streak) and 28 achievements. Bought embers never count.

No ads, no tracking

No account, no ads, no analytics, and it plays fully offline.

Descriptions

One line

Scorchlings is a pixel puzzle game for iPhone and iPad where baby dragons burn away pictures one color at a time, across 1,000 levels and with no ads, ever.

Short paragraph

Scorchlings is a color puzzle game for iPhone and iPad. Each baby dragon breathes one color of fire: players send dragons around a pixel picture, and each one burns the tiles of its color it can see, while leftover dragons wait in a small number of roosts. Over 1,000 levels the pictures grow into full boards with tricky shades, and new twists arrive: chained dragons, mystery dragons, frozen dragons and stone walls. Five power-ups, a daily gift calendar and Game Center leaderboards round it out. The first 100 levels are free, and one purchase opens the rest. There are no ads, no accounts and no tracking. Scorchlings was built by independent developer Alexander Rohr.
